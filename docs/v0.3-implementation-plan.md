# Gogogo v0.3–v0.6 — Implementation Plan
**Status:** implementation-ready specification; planning only, no gameplay changes in this commit.
**Baseline:** main at `784078d6ffed36a673aa49163f328f5d387c105d` (2026-10-30).
**Primary platforms:** GitHub Pages, desktop browser, touch/mobile browser, self-contained offline HTML.
**Primary experience:** colorful, high-quality voxel taxi adventure; Balanced Adventure mountain driving; simple kids' driving HUD.
**Delivery approach:** vertical slices and release gates; each milestone ends in a demonstrably playable build.

## 1. Non-negotiable contracts

1. **One connected world.** City, Pinecrest Hills, countryside, Coral Bay beach and Skyport must be joined by actual driveable roads, physically continuous terrain and connected navigation. No fake cross-country straight-line route.
2. **Fixed landmarks, stable identities.** Protect IDs, footprints, entrances, designs and world coordinates for Clock Square (-150,150), Metro Terminal (150,150), Welcome Gate (0,-27), Sunrise Plaza (30,30), Pinecrest Lodge (206,470) and Skyport Airport (444,-114). New beach landmarks must enter the same immutable registry. Use plot-reservation checks before creating assets and collision shapes. Do not change existing landmark identity merely to improve decoration.
3. **Saved progress remains readable.** Current storage key is `voxel-city-driver:v1`; current schema is `version:1`, with cash, earnings, trips, owned/selected cars, car colors, position, heading, hasPlayed and merged settings. Keep loading old saves, preserve ownership/purchases, make new settings additive and default-safe. If a real schema migration becomes necessary, add explicit migration tests; never silently reset saves.
4. **Render/collision agreement.** Ground mesh and physics agree at the same vertices; collision-backed beaches and roads use exactly the same triangle data or intentionally matched heightfield. Do not hide collision gaps with non-visual floating slabs. The existing y=-9 catch floor remains only last-resort recovery, never valid ground support.
5. **No exaggerated jumping.** Maintain bounded per-wheel spring-damper assistance, ground-contact tests, pitch/roll damping and vertical-velocity limits. Never add decorative rocks colliders inside driveable lanes. Wind and rain must not reintroduce wheelies or launches.
6. **Style discipline.** Premium, warm, colorful voxel diorama with intentional compositions and memorable structures; not repetitive boxes, flat water sheets or generic circular potholes. Source artwork/assets must be original or appropriately licensed. Repeated geometry should be instanced or merged; distant silhouettes should have LOD.
7. **Kid-friendly controls.** Keep speed, next turn/distance, small minimap, touch controls and pickup interaction; avoid extra gameplay text and control overlap. Preserve accessible input labels.
8. **Offline viability.** Core game and all required art/logic must ship without CDNs, runtime APIs or cloud dependencies. Offer GitHub Pages and an offline build from the same code/version. iPhone Files preview cannot be assumed to execute a local HTML file; test the installable offline-PWA path separately.

## 2. Current architecture and known gaps (verified at baseline)

- `World.js` builds the terrain, uniform road grid, procedural city, airport, lake and landmarks. `SurfaceSystem.js` supplies `terrainHeight` and road-type queries.
- The world collider footprint is **1320 x 1000 units** with **132 x 100 heightfield cells** (10-unit spacing). This is too coarse for local coastal shorelines, narrow curbs or potholes. There is a targeted terrain triangle collision patch in `MountainSurfacePatch.js` and more detailed collidable road patches in `RegionalRoads.js` and `RoadDamage.js`.
- The rural road in `RegionalRoads.js` has visual/physics meshes but is **not yet connected to the general route graph**. `Route.js` currently connects the city, airport spur and Pinecrest route. Solve this before adding another unconnected coastal road.
- The existing decorative lake is a flat circle near **(-325,335)**. Do not treat it as the beach, displace it without a migration, or accidentally overlap its footprint.
- `Missions.js` currently selects a pickup and always delivers to Skyport Airport. It requires route-valid pickup placement; adding beach pickup records to `PICKUPS` without verifying reachability is not sufficient.
- Current menus live in `UI.js` and CSS; the title currently says “VOXEL CITY DRIVER,” and the garage uses symbols/cards rather than in-engine car previews. The desired release identity is **Gogogo**.
- GitHub Actions runs `npm run check`, Vite build, Playwright browser smoke and deploys to Pages. Automated captures already include city, mountain, damaged-road, countryside and mobile screenshots.
- Current performance targets are aspirations, **not measured device benchmarks**. Profiling must be recorded before calling a milestone optimized.

## 5. P1 — Phase 3A: Coral Bay coastal foundation

### Proposed placement (validate, do not blindly hardcode)

Use the **southwest outskirts** instead of the existing northwest lake. Initial candidate coastal envelope: x in [-620,-330], z in [-460,-230]. The sea lies predominantly west of a gently curving shoreline; cliffs and sandy coves occupy the inner coast. The proposed city feeder starts at the existing southwest city grid, around (-240,-240), then curves through (-305,-265), (-370,-292), (-420,-310) toward a waterfront turnaround around (-490,-340). These are **planning anchors**; final positions require intersection, slope, boundary and footprint checks.

The current terrain has physical bounds x[-660,660], z[-500,500]; the camera can see decorative water outside them, but the car may not drive on unsupported ground. Reserve a recovery boundary before the open sea. The coastline must not touch Skyport's apron or the protected downtown/mountain assets.

### Work items

**P1.1 / Coast layout:** Add `CoastLayout.js` with a versioned coastline spline/polyline, masks, authored road centerline, defined safe coast boundary, shoreline normals, reserved landmark parcels and stable IDs. Create a plotting/debug mode that displays reserved envelopes and the proposed route.

**P1.2 / Terrain:** Add `CoastalTerrain.js`. Blend regional beach height and dunes with the existing `terrainHeight` at a sufficiently broad buffer (no ledge where coast begins). Cut the shoreline into real geometry; keep the physically driveable sand and asphalt above water. Introduce local high-resolution meshes/tiles near the sea and road only; do not simply increase the entire 132x100 world grid.

**P1.3 / Road:** Add `CoastalRoad.js`, using road splines, graded turns and low-curvature shoulders. Use the same triangle vertices for visible road and Rapier collider (existing road patches are the pattern), with continuously graded start/end transitions. Add beach-road markings, drainage and carefully chosen, shallow worn sections, not uniform potholes everywhere. Provide barriers or realistic recovery on seawall edges, with room for car width.

**P1.4 / Navigation:** Register all coast connectors and coastal waypoints in the common graph (from P0). Test actual centerline continuity and bidirectional shortest paths from city, Pinecrest, airport and valley. Road guide chevrons must follow the spline, not a shortcut across the sea.

**P1.5 / Vehicle surfaces:** Sand uses modestly reduced grip and softer acceleration/drag. Coastal asphalt remains comfortable. The kid-friendly default must not beach the car merely for touching sand; maintain recoverable boundaries and safe spawn points. Use the existing bounded spring model.

**G1 pass:** City-to-coast drive is entirely on real surfaces; no colliders missing at transitions; no floating road slabs or ocean-through-ground artifacts; the car can navigate curves and turn around, return to city, recover if rolled, and stay under established tilt/vertical-launch limits. Screenshots show recognizable shore topology even before final art.

**P2.3 / Effects and optimization:** Instanced palms, umbrellas, seating and boats, cheap swaying foliage material, foam tile LOD, geometry merging where appropriate. Water sound and effects must be local assets or generated in-browser; no network dependencies.

**P2.4 / Missions:** Add 1-2 optional beach passenger pickup sites with exact parking/pickup anchors on reachable terrain. Preserve current taxi-to-airport mission behavior unless separately approved for variable destinations. Use familiar capacity/reward progression; prevent passenger markers from floating above beach, standing underwater or colliding with props.

**P2.5 / Weather hooks:** Expose wind and wetness uniform inputs for later P3 without making weather a hard requirement for P2.

**G2 pass:** lighthouse/pier/cafes persist with fixed design/position; taxi can follow a valid route, collect passengers, deliver to Skyport, save progress and return; visible waves and shore foam remain stable on low graphics; shoreline looks visibly different from the city's existing lake.

## 7. P3 — Phase 4: Wind, rain and atmospheric weather

**P3.1 / `WeatherSystem.js`:** State machine with `mode: auto|sunny|rainy`, `effectsEnabled`, `intensity`, `windVector`, `wetness`, `transitionProgress`, optional time-of-day. Auto weather uses a seeded, in-game timeline (not live weather APIs). Weather must resume predictably or reset intentionally; never corrupt progress. Add new settings via defaults merged into the existing v1 save.

**P3.2 / `WeatherEffects.js`:** Low-cost camera-centered rain particles (visual system follows player; no rain across whole map), moving leaves/dust, tree and palm sway, subtle particle splashes near wheels, distant fog over mountains. Quality scale low/medium/high with explicit effect caps.

**P3.3 / Materials:** Wet-road darkening and bounded highlights; puddle meshes appear in verified geometric low points, especially actual potholes, not randomly floating circles. Sand darkens near shoreline or in rain. Separate weather overlay from collision geometry so the road still has the same real pothole depth.

**P3.4 / Physics:** Grip changes should be controlled and mild in kid-friendly driving; per-wheel forces stay within anti-launch caps. Fog/rain must never conceal the next turn, barriers, pedestrians or on-road guide arrows.

**P3.5 / UX:** Settings provide simple Auto / Sunny / Rainy choice, optional effects-reduced/off control (prefer disable particles while preserving optional environmental lighting), and quality fallback on lower-end devices. A small weather cue may appear in settings, not another crowded driving HUD label.

**G3 pass:** clear-to-rain-to-clear visibly transitions with no flicker or rubber-banding; puddles align with ground; wind direction matches foliage/rain/waves; rainy hill and beach routes pass braking and collision tests; mobile frame-time regression within agreed budget.

## 9. P5 — Phase 6: End-to-end integration and release QA

**Test matrix:** desktop Chromium (at least 1280x720), 844x390 mobile landscape, 390x844 portrait, 320x640 narrow, and real iPhone Safari and representative Android Chrome where hardware is available. CI browser evidence is necessary but cannot establish iPhone quality alone.

**Physics and navigation scenarios:**
- 10+ minutes of continuous throttle, braking, reversing, sharp turning and collision; no violent wheelies, repeated large jumps or falling below valid terrain.
- City -> Pinecrest -> city; city -> countryside -> city; city -> Coral Bay -> Skyport; full pickups, currency, ownership and reload.
- Road seam sampling along each generated route and at every crossing, with validated collider hits **near the visible ground**, not the emergency floor. Camera line-of-sight and clip/occlusion tests against houses/trees.
- Every fixed landmark's id/position/footprint and entry route remains unchanged; every object that appears solid has an appropriate collision profile or documented decorative-only status.
- Weather-off vs heavy-rain visual and physical tests; dry vs wet braking distances should be believable but kid-friendly. No HUD obscuration.
- Old `version:1` saves load unchanged and return to safe coordinates. New world content never overwrites paid-for vehicles or earnings.

**Visual captures:** same actual chase camera for city, damaged road, mountain, valley, beach; sunset/rain variants; title, pause, settings and 3D garage; mobile landscape/portrait screens. Review lighting, roads, obstacle scale, graphics quality and readability, not just that screenshots exist.

**Performance instrumentation:** log render FPS, 95th-percentile frame time, draw calls, triangle count, texture/material usage and memory where available. Initial goals: **30 FPS on supported midrange mobile**, **60 FPS desktop**, mobile p95 frame time <=33.3 ms and desktop p95 <=16.7 ms in representative scenes. Treat these as **targets**, to be validated against named test hardware. Heavy rain, town center and lodge/country transitions must be included. Use instancing, LOD, object pools, effect caps and pixel-ratio scaling instead of blanking scenes.

**Offline release:** Automate building an inline, self-contained HTML from the *same successful release* used by GitHub Pages. Include CSS/JS/WASM and locally bundled resources; no runtime CDN/remote fetches. Prefer a service-worker-cached installable PWA for iOS/offline Home Screen as a separate checked pathway. Test full offline gameplay after cache/download, not merely that the first screen opens. Publish a ZIP and release notes with each green milestone.

**CI and release sequence:** snapshot main -> implement in coherent commits -> unit tests -> production build -> real WebGL/WASM browser smoke -> required screenshots -> visual review -> relevant mobile hardware checks -> Pages publish -> archive the matching offline build. Keep last known-good release accessible; do not treat a red build as deployed.

**G5 pass:** All scenarios green, visually reviewed, settings/save compatibility verified, performance budget measured and accepted, GitHub Pages live, matching offline artifact verified for its supported environments.

## 10. Implementation contracts and ownership boundaries

`WorldManifest.js`: stable authored IDs, transforms, footprints, entrances, collision profiles and reserved parcels.

`SurfaceSystem.js` + `CoastalTerrain.js`: a single authoritative `surfaceAt(x,z)` including height, type and driveability; compatible `terrainHeight` wrapper until consumers migrate.

`CoastalRoad.js` + `RoadNetwork.js`: collision-backed road ribbon geometry and graph edges in one definition. Road material and physical position must agree.

`OceanSystem.js`: visual sea, foam and tide/shoreline animation; no road or safety physics inside a purely visual ocean plane.

`WeatherSystem.js`: deterministic world weather; does not own UI or driver controls.

`WeatherEffects.js`: effect pools, material inputs, particle caps; no expensive per-frame mesh allocation.

`BeachDistrict.js`: stable authored models, decorative instancing, optional collidable objects and pickup anchors.

`UITheme.js` / `GaragePreview.js`: styling and 3D preview lifecycle; never own saved-game mutations (continue through existing game methods).

`WorldDiagnostics.js`: local debug visualizations and automatic checks for terrain seams, road graph components, protected landmark overlap, collider depth, performance, missing assets and non-finite geometry.

**Dependency order:** manifest/road graph/surface contract -> coast terrain -> coast road and physics -> sea and assets -> beach mission -> weather -> UI -> whole-world QA. UI art prototypes may run concurrently but UI integration must not destabilize mission gameplay.

## 11. Risk register and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Coast extends beyond 1000-unit terrain depth | Car falls into void | Restrict drivable footprint; local shore colliders; safe edge/recovery test |
| Beach or shoreline overlaps lake, city or landmarks | Lost visual consistency | Preflight reserved-plot checks with map and screenshot diff |
| Floating road/water graphics over coarse heightfield | Fake driving/vehicle jumps | Local render-physics shared triangles; raycasts to visible surface |
| Unconnected coastline route graph | Misleading GPS arrows | Graph-component tests; no straight-line fallback for playable targets |
| Weather doubles draw calls and causes mobile stutter | Poor user experience | Instancing, pooled particles, profile-based budgets, scene-aware throttling |
| Additive features corrupt old saves | User loses cars/progress | Stable storage key, merged settings, migration and reload tests |
| Garage rendering creates a second expensive game loop | Mobile slowdown | Reuse assets/renderer, pause hidden scene and benchmark |
| Too much UI text | Bad kids' usability | Fixed HUD inventory and viewport overlap screenshots |
| Visual consistency deteriorates as content expands | City feels generic | Fixed landmark style guide, authored hero assets, deterministic parcel seeds |
| Offline HTML works only on desktop | User cannot play offline on iPhone | Separate tested offline PWA + candid device-specific instructions |

## 12. Definition of Done for every task

A work item is complete only when: (a) source and tests are committed, (b) gameplay starts with no console errors, (c) its actual render/physics/navigation claims are verified, (d) the relevant screenshot is inspected, (e) no existing landmark or old save changes unexpectedly, (f) controls work in desktop and mobile layouts, and (g) CI and Pages deployment are green. Report remaining limitations explicitly.

## 14. Roadside realism and traffic challenge — approved expansion (2026-10-30)

**Problem statement:** The current world feels too blank. The player can frequently drive around traffic on open terrain, bypass congestion, and reach destinations without meaningful road decisions. The implementation must make the roads and their surroundings feel like a coherent lived-in place, while retaining fair, optional off-road exploration.

**Verified baseline:** `src/traffic/Traffic.js` currently creates 22 kinematic vehicles on eight authored city loops, with basic player/vehicle following and alternating intersection priority. Mountain/country scenery has limited collidable rocks and trees, but there is no consistent roadside containment or region-specific traffic population. These are baseline observations, not completed improvements.

### 14.1 Delivery order and dependencies

| Stage | Priority | Implementation | Depends on | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 — Roadside design contract | Next alongside P1 | Road classification, clearance envelopes, collision and parcel reservations | P0 layout/graph | B0 |
| B1 — Coast and country natural boundaries | Immediately after playable P1 coast ribbon | Ditches, culverts, boulders, tree lines, fences, dunes, seawalls | P1 collision-backed roads | B1 |
| B2 — City and airport edges | Before traffic density increase | Footpaths, compound walls, drains, parking, barriers, junction visibility | B0 | B2 |
| T1 — Regional traffic foundation | After B1/B2 collision tests | Traffic zones, spawn budgets, mixed vehicle profiles, deterministic pooling | B1/B2 | T1 |
| T2 — Responsive traffic behaviour | After T1 | Queueing, adaptive braking, lane following, yielding, turn speeds, collision avoidance | T1 | T2 |
| T3 — Regional difficulty and challenges | After T2 | Downtown congestion, country passing, mountain caution, beach queues, airport drop-offs | T2 + P2 beach missions | T3 |

**Scheduling decision:** Keep P1 coastal road as the next playable milestone. Implement B0 alongside it and B1 as part of the first coast/country driving review. Do not postpone *all* natural boundaries until the final polish phase. Implement B2 before increasing city traffic density. Weather and UI can progress after the road/traffic stability gates, but neither should mask a physically empty world.

### 14.2 B0 — RoadsideProfile and clearance contracts

Create `src/world/RoadsideProfile.js` describing profiles for **urban boulevard, residential street, industrial/airport, countryside, mountain, and coast**. Profiles define road width, shoulders, pedestrian space, safe recoverable off-road margin, obstacle zones, drainage side, visibility setbacks and suitable prop types.

Use the actual city grid and authored mountain, countryside and coastal centerlines. For each sampled segment, derive tangent, normal, terrain slope and left/right roadside bands. Keep solid objects beyond a vehicle-width clearance envelope, especially near sharp turns and taxi pickup points. Use `WorldManifest.js` protected footprints and mission anchors as exclusion zones. Deterministic seeds must keep scenery stable across reloads.

**Physics contract:** Every rock, wall, guardrail, trunk or ditch that visually obstructs a car must have a corresponding collision or deliberately documented traversable behavior. Never place an invisible collider inside a drivable lane. Ditches and open naalas need visibly lowered terrain/channel geometry with matching Rapier collision; a painted dark stripe is not sufficient. All crossings require an actual culvert, bridge or graded driveway. No generic infinite invisible walls.

### 14.3 B1 — Natural boundaries for country, mountain and beach

- **Countryside:** Narrow shoulders, irregular rocky banks, boulders, thick shrubs, field hedges, fence lines, small drainage ditches and culverts. Occasional realistic pull-offs and field entrances. Dense vegetation should block easy straight-line shortcuts, not block legitimate road curves.
- **Mountains:** Rock cuttings, uneven slopes, large trees, fallen rocks, ravines and guardrails only where a drop demands them. Vehicle can still pull over at designated viewpoints. Maintain Balanced Adventure anti-launch physics and camera transparency around obscuring vegetation.
- **Beach:** Sand dunes, coastal vegetation, boulders, erosion edges, drains, occasional cliffs and selective seawalls. The road must have safe turning/parking near the lighthouse, pier and market. No ocean crossing shortcuts or collider gaps at shoreline transitions.
- **Physical quality:** Build collision-backed roadside terrain strips from shared render/physics vertices; instanced small details; limited individual colliders for large props; camera visibility checks. Ditches should slow or trap an incautious car in a recoverable way, never cause a sudden launch or permanent stuck state.

**B1 acceptance:** Test driving beside the road and attempting to cut across at multiple points. Off-road traversal should sometimes be possible but no longer consistently faster than the road. No solid prop penetration, no spontaneous flips, no trapped taxi mission points, and valid city-to-coast-to-city navigation.

### 14.4 B2 — City and airport boundaries

- **Urban streets:** Pavements, raised curbs, open stormwater drains/naalas where appropriate, shopfront steps, residential compound walls, gates, parked vehicles, utility poles and trees. Create recognizably different commercial, residential and industrial blocks instead of repeating the same decoration.
- **Intersections:** Keep pedestrian crossings, turn radii, sightlines and lane clearance free of randomly spawned colliders. Drains crossing road entries require a physical culvert. Do not surround every city block with identical continuous walls.
- **Airport:** Security fences, access roads, curb islands, controlled entry points, parking and taxi pickup/drop-off queues, while protecting the existing airport building/runway and passenger destination.
- **Visual density:** Combine authored signature assets with procedural variation and instancing. Empty roadside space should be an intentional park, field or plaza rather than undecorated ground.

**B2 acceptance:** Downtown and suburban screenshot review, vehicle collision clearance, route continuity, landmark protection, accessible passenger markers and stable mobile frame time.

### 14.5 T1 — Traffic distribution and mixed fleet

Refactor `src/traffic/Traffic.js` to use a zone/road-graph-aware traffic controller with deterministic seeds, density caps and spawn/despawn safety checks. Support compact cars, taxis, buses, delivery trucks, autos and two-wheelers with correct physical dimensions, visual models and speed/turn envelopes. Mix parked and moving vehicles; do not place parked cars in the center of a lane. Spawn no vehicle inside the player, a protected landmark, a pedestrian crossing or a pickup trigger.

Start with a measured baseline (22 moving vehicles); increase *effective visible density* via region-aware spawning, route overlap and intersection queues rather than an arbitrary world-wide multiplication. Define `low / normal / busy` traffic settings, with device-quality-aware caps. Preserve save compatibility and avoid new external services.

### 14.6 T2 — Traffic behavior

Implement lane-aware following distance, smooth acceleration/braking, predictive stopping behind player and other NPCs, intersection occupancy reservations, yielding, stop-line compliance, turn-speed reductions and queue recovery. NPCs must react to collisions and obstructions; never continue moving through the player's car. Avoid deadlocks through deterministic release rules and timed re-evaluation. Distinguish overtaking where physically safe from cutting through roadside terrain. Emergency braking should be strong enough to avoid routine rear-end crashes without instant teleport-like stops.

**T2 acceptance:** Automated intersection conflict tests, queue start/stop tests, player-stopped-in-lane tests, opposing traffic tests and 10-minute stress drives. No recurrent ghost-driving, unfair ramming, persistent gridlock or major performance regression.

### 14.7 T3 — Region-specific challenge

| Region | Traffic and road feel |
|---|---|
| Downtown | Busy junctions, parked cars, taxis, buses, delivery stops and moderate congestion |
| Residential | Slower local cars, children/pedestrian caution, speed humps and narrow entrances |
| Countryside | Fewer vehicles but narrow lanes, roadside ditches, slower trucks and limited passing opportunities |
| Mountains | Careful cornering, reduced sightlines, occasional oncoming vehicles and rockfall pull-offs |
| Coral Bay | Light approach traffic, concentrated parking and queues around beach market and pier |
| Airport | Arrival/departure queues, shuttles and drop-off traffic with controlled curb access |

Challenges should come from **road width, sightlines, traffic behavior and environmental constraints**, not from unavoidable crashes. Keep recovery/tow available and optional difficulty settings. Do not add fines or mandatory timers without explicit product approval.

### 14.8 Test matrix and release gates

1. **B0:** Validate segment clearance bands, no protected plot or pickup overlap, road geometry and collision matching.
2. **B1:** Coast/country/mountain screenshots and real driving; no easy universal terrain bypass; intentional off-road routes still possible.
3. **B2:** City/airport collision and accessibility; no blocked turns, building doors or taxi triggers.
